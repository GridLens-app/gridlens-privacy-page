GridLens

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 30 October 2026 · Applies to the GridLens app for Android (com.gridlens.app)

GridLens helps you draw from a photo by laying a grid over it. This page explains what the app does with your information. The short version: your pictures and projects stay on your phone, and the only outside service the app talks to is Google’s ad service, which is used for one optional video.

At a glance

  • No account, no sign-in, and no GridLens server. We never receive your photos, projects or exports.
  • GridLens has no analytics or crash-reporting tools of its own.
  • The camera (only if you choose to use the camera overlay) is shown live on screen and is never recorded, saved or sent.
  • Larger exports and the camera overlay can be unlocked by watching a short rewarded video from Google AdMob. That is the only reason the app goes online, and Google handles the data it collects for it (see “Ads”).

1. What stays on your device

Everything you create in GridLens is stored on your phone, in the app’s private storage:

  • the pictures you open and your projects (grid, lines, colours, filters, crops and edit history);
  • an untouched copy of each picture you import, kept inside the project so edits can be undone. If your photo contains hidden details such as the location where it was taken, those details stay inside that copy on your phone. GridLens does not read them and does not send them anywhere;
  • your settings, such as language, display size, haptics, and which tips you have hidden;
  • a few small counters for the rating pop-up (number of launches, when it was last shown, whether you rated).

The app is excluded from Android’s cloud backup, so this information is not copied to your Google account by the app. Uninstalling GridLens, or deleting a project inside the app, removes it from your phone.

When you export, GridLens saves the file where you tell it to (for example Pictures/GridLens). Files you export or share with another app are in your hands from then on. GridLens does not upload them.

2. Camera

The camera overlay shows your gridded picture over a live camera view. The camera permission is requested only when you tap “Allow camera” on that screen, never when the app starts. The camera image is displayed on screen and is not recorded, stored or sent. The “Compare” snapshot is held in the phone’s memory only and is discarded when you close the screen. You can withdraw the permission at any time in your phone’s settings; every other part of the app keeps working.

3. Ads (Google AdMob)

GridLens contains one kind of ad: an optional rewarded video. Watching it unlocks the larger export sizes and poster printing for the open project, or the live camera overlay for one hour. Normal-size exports never need a video. The ads are provided by Google AdMob, and the app asks for ads suitable for most audiences (rating “PG” or lower).

When the ad service runs, Google may collect and use information from your device, such as your IP address (which can hint at your general location), your advertising ID or app set ID, details about your device and the app, technical performance and diagnostic information (for example how quickly the app starts and whether it freezes), and how you interact with the ad. This happens under Google’s own policies, not ours:

  • How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services
  • Google Privacy Policy

When does the app contact Google’s ad service? Nothing ad-related runs when the app opens. A few seconds after you open a project, the app may start the ad service quietly, but only where no consent form is needed or you have already answered it. Where the law requires a consent form (the EU, the UK and Switzerland), the form is shown only when a video is actually needed, never in the middle of editing. People who never open a project, and never choose a feature that needs a video, do not cause any contact with Google’s ad service.

Your choices. You can say no to a video and still use every free feature. Where the law requires it, Settings shows an “Ad privacy choices” row that reopens the consent form. On your phone you can also reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of ads personalisation, in Android’s Settings under Google, then Ads.

4. Rating and feedback

  • Rating. “Rate now” opens Google Play’s own review sheet. Google Play handles your rating under its own policies, and GridLens does not see what you write.
  • Send feedback. This opens your own email app with our address, a subject and a short footer (app version, Android version, and your phone’s make and model) already filled in. Nothing is sent until you press Send. If you do, we receive your email address and what you wrote, and we use it only to reply and to fix problems.

5. Permissions the app asks for

PermissionWhy
Internet and network stateOnly for the rewarded video and its consent form, and for the Play review sheet.
Advertising IDUsed by Google’s ad service, as described in section 3.
Camera (optional)Only for the live camera overlay, asked when you tap “Allow camera”. Tablets and other devices without a camera can still install the app.

GridLens does not ask for access to your storage, contacts, location or microphone. It picks pictures with Android’s own photo picker or share sheet, which gives it only the picture you choose.

6. What we do not do

  • We do not collect, store or have access to your photos, projects or exports.
  • We do not sell your information, and we do not share it with anyone, because we do not receive it.
  • We do not use your information for profiling or to build an advertising profile of you. Anything of that kind is done by Google within its ad service, under the choices described above.

7. Children

GridLens is a general drawing aid and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. Ads are limited to content suitable for most audiences.

8. Your rights

Because your pictures, projects and settings live only on your phone, you control them directly: you can view, change, export or delete them in the app, or remove everything by uninstalling it. For data that Google’s ad service handles, use Google’s own tools and the choices in section 3. If you have a question or a request about your privacy, write to us at the address below.

9. Changes to this policy

If the app changes in a way that affects your privacy, for example a new feature that uses other services, we will update this page and the date at the top before the change reaches you. Please check back from time to time.
